# Should open Bounties use Contract slots?

Status: ready-for-agent
Category: enhancement

Open Bounties count against the board’s Contract cap and never expire. Each
standing unanswered Bounty on an active Lair can occupy a slot indefinitely;
enough Bounties prevent new Contracts and raids from reaching the board.

This is questionable because work to recover Holdings competes with standing
Lair work, so companies can lose access to Contracts until a Bounty is taken.

Possible rules:

- Keep one shared cap for all open work and describe it as the board’s work cap.
- Count only Contracts against the existing cap; allow uncapped Bounties.
- Give Contracts and Bounties separate caps or reserved board slots.

Leave the current behaviour unchanged until the rule is decided.

### Agent Brief (2026-10-12)

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Only open Contracts count against the Board's cap of open work; Bounties stand outside it.

**Current behavior:**
`world.maxOpenQuests` (default 8) is compared with every open work on the Board, Contracts and Bounties together, before an employer posts a Contract at the hourly step and before a raid posts one. Bounties never expire and there is at most one per active lair, so each unanswered Bounty holds one of those places for as long as its lair stands. The guild's Bounty posting does not read the cap at all: a Bounty is posted on a full Board, and from then on it keeps a Contract out.

**Desired behavior:**
The owner chose the second rule of the issue. The cap counts open Contracts only. An open Bounty does not take a place, whatever their number, and Bounties have no cap of their own: they stay limited to one per active lair, posted under the same conditions as today. With eight open Contracts no further Contract is posted, by the hourly step or by a raid; with seven open Contracts and any number of open Bounties, one more is.

`world.maxOpenQuests` keeps its name, its default and its validation; the documents describe it as the cap of open Contracts. No random draw is added or removed by the rule itself, but more Contracts reach the Board, so the default game changes and the regression hashes are re-recorded once, after the tests below pass.

**Key interfaces:**
- The two places of the game that compare open work with `world.maxOpenQuests` (hourly Contract posting and raid posting) — count Contracts only.
- The Board — if it needs a way to tell open Contracts from open Bounties, it uses the kind of work it already records; no new link between employer and work.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] With the cap's worth of open Contracts on the Board, neither the hourly step nor a raid posts another Contract.
- [ ] With one Contract fewer than the cap and three open Bounties, the hourly step posts a Contract; before this change it did not.
- [ ] The same case holds for a Contract posted by a raid.
- [ ] A Bounty is posted on a Board that holds the cap's worth of open Contracts, as today.
- [ ] The regression hashes are re-recorded in this change and the report says which tests changed and why; no other seeded expectation is edited without naming it in the report.
- [ ] The tuning comparison of `scripts/tune.ts` against `scripts/tune-baseline.json` is run and its output recorded in the Comments. If a metric leaves its band, no tuning value is moved, `world.maxOpenQuests` included: the output and the metric are reported and the issue is left for the owner.
- [ ] `docs/CONFIGURATION.md` describes `world.maxOpenQuests` as the cap of open Contracts; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `CHANGELOG.md` describe the rule; `CONTEXT.md` is changed only if it describes the cap.
- [ ] `pnpm typecheck` and `pnpm test` are green.

**Out of scope:**
- An expiry for Bounties, and a cap of their own.
- Changing the value of `world.maxOpenQuests` or any other tuning value.
- The Reward set-aside over Bounties (potions-and-short-rest issue 11).
